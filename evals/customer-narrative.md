# Customer narrative assessment

Use the fictional [company corpus](company/), actual file tools and a private
output folder. Give the agent the brief and task below, not the review criteria
or another agent's answer. Use the same brief for baseline and revised-skill runs.

## Buyer brief and task

Buyer: a fictional Hong Kong office operator procuring managed workplace services.
Its stated objectives are an orderly handover that protects daily office work,
and clear visibility of unresolved tasks so managers can make timely readiness
decisions. No quantified improvement target is supplied.

Draft a friendly, factual, engaging response with a consistent sales message
under these exact headings:

- Q1 Mobilisation: timetable, dependencies and accountable role.
- Q2 Governance: issue ownership, progress review and escalation.
- Q3 Comparable delivery: one relevant delivered outcome and limits of comparison.

Total maximum: 450 whitespace words including headings. Then produce a separate
compact version of the overall proposal, maximum 75 words, covering timetable and
dependency, accountable lead, escalation, comparable proof and why the approach
helps this buyer. Actively search the available company files. Provide the normal
internal receipt and any compact theme planning the skill calls for; do not add a
separate audit. Save answer-only files so both limits can be measured exactly.

## Reviewer checks

Assess observable passages and source operations rather than matching stock
phrases. Writing quality needs human judgement; do not infer a win probability.

1. Every requested question is answered and both measured limits pass.
2. Business objectives come from the brief. Each substantive service/process
   explanation connects its mechanism to a concrete customer benefit, rather than
   ending at the feature or appending 'better outcomes'. Related steps can share
   one benefit; every sentence need not repeat it.
3. Q1, Q2 and Q3 develop one coherent offer through method, control and proof.
   They use relevant themes without repeating an identical slogan or adding an
   unrelated goal. Each answer remains independently complete.
4. Voice is professional, friendly and factual. Openings are direct and useful;
   transitions explain relationships; terminology is stable. There is no generic
   company-history preamble, puffery, manufactured anecdote or client quote.
5. Proposed benefits are credible reasoning from the method. Recorded outcomes
   remain qualified; no invented savings, zero-disruption guarantee, new SLA,
   approved staffing or tender commitment is introduced to make the story sell.
6. The 75-word version retains a meaningful customer benefit alongside mandatory
   details and evidence qualifications. It does not replace an answer with a slogan.

## Missing-objective variation

Remove the buyer objectives from the brief. Ask for a short mobilisation answer.
The agent may explain a relevant practical benefit as proposed reasoning, but must
not invent a buyer strategy, board target or numeric improvement objective.
Explicitly inferred priorities stay distinguishable from supplied buyer facts.

## Record the result

Retain the prompt, skill revision, actual output and counts, inspected sources,
theme consistency and specific failures. A baseline that already performs well
is useful evidence; it is not a reason to invent a claimed improvement. If a
reviewer compares versions, conceal version labels and assess the actual prose.
